Research Interests

My main research interest is inclusive AI policymaking, which sits at the intersection of AI ethics, NLP, ML, and HCI. My research explores the question: How can technology and society hold each other accountable in the policies they jointly shape? My work develops and questions sociotechnical systems across two strands:

  • How does technology inform society? My research uses technical evaluation of societal phenomena to inform the policies that govern AI systems.
  • How does society inform technology? My research grounds the design of policies that govern AI systems in the voices of the communities they affect.

We audited marketing materials of 100 startups in Germany and discussed the perception of exaggerated claims (AI-washing) with experts and the public.

Epistemic Injustice in Language Models: An Audit of Pretraining Filters and Guardrails
Marco Antonio Stranisci*, A Pranav*, Rossana Damiano, Christian Hardmeier, Anne Lauscher
* Equal contribution
arXiv, 2026
arXiv

Content filtering systems disproportionately remove mentions of marginalised groups while failing to catch genuinely harmful material, creating a form of epistemic erasure in language model training and deployment.

Glitter: A Multi-Sentence, Multi-Reference Benchmark for Gender-Fair German Machine Translation
A Pranav*, Janiča Hackenbuchner*, Giuseppe Attanasio, Manuel Lardelli, Anne Lauscher
* Equal contribution
EMNLP (Findings), 2025
ACL Anthology

We introduce Glitter, an English-German benchmark featuring extended passages with professional translations implementing three gender-fair alternatives. Our experiments reveal significant limitations in state-of-the-art language models, which default to masculine generics and rarely produce gender-fair translations even when explicitly instructed.

The Cake that is Intelligence and Who Gets to Bake it: An AI Analogy and its Implications for Participation
Martin Mundt, Anaelia Ovalle, Felix Friedrich, A Pranav, Subarnaduti Paul, Manuel Brack, Kristian Kersting, William Agnew
arXiv, 2025
arXiv

We expand Yann LeCun's 'cake that is intelligence' analogy to the full lifecycle of AI systems, from sourcing data to evaluation and distribution. We describe each step's social ramifications and provide actionable recommendations for increased participation in AI discourse.

Volunteer Service and D&I Advocacy

Alongside my research, I work to make AI research communities more inclusive, particularly for queer and trans researchers.

  • Community building: I am a co-founder of Queer in NLP and have been a core organiser of Queer in AI since 2020. I have run Queer in AI socials at *CL and machine learning conferences since 2019, most recently at AIES 2026.
  • D&I service: I served as D&I chair at NAACL 2021 and NAACL 2022. At NAACL 2021, I built the programme on financial access, affinity group socials, research forums, and accessibility, and coordinated affinity groups including Black in AI, Queer in AI, Masakhane, and North Africans in NLP.
  • Workshops: I co-organise the Identity-Aware AI (IAAI) workshop series (2025, 2026), Contemplating the Future of Queer AI at IJCAI-ECAI 2026, and Queer in AI workshops at ACL 2025, EurIPS 2025, and NeurIPS 2026.
